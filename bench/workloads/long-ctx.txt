The North Quay maintenance log uses a deliberately redundant format so that later readers can reconstruct causality without external references. Each inspection starts at the brass clock, proceeds through the east corridor, records air pressure, hinge vibration, lamp color, relay temperature, and the exact order of audible signals. A blue lamp means the circuit was inspected but not reset. An amber lamp means a reset occurred after a manual measurement. A white lamp means two technicians independently obtained the same value. No lamp color describes severity.

During ordinary operation, the sequence bell, click, bell marks a completed relay cycle. Bell, bell, click marks an interrupted cycle awaiting confirmation. Click, bell, click is only a calibration signature and must never be counted as an alarm. When a written entry conflicts with the signal trace, the signal trace controls timing, while the written entry controls the stated purpose. This distinction matters because several older ledgers use the word emergency for scheduled drills.

Mira recorded cabinet A at 02:10 with pressure 1008.4 hPa, hinge vibration 0.7 mm/s, a blue lamp, and bell-click-bell. Leon recorded cabinet B at 02:14 with pressure 1008.2 hPa, hinge vibration 0.8 mm/s, an amber lamp, and click-bell-click. Cabinet C was not opened; its white lamp was inherited from the previous shift and therefore cannot establish a second measurement for this inspection. At 02:21 the corridor microphone captured bell-bell-click from cabinet D, but the written entry at 02:23 called the event a weekly drill.

For audit summaries, state observations before interpretations. Preserve all timestamps, distinguish inherited indicators from indicators set during the current inspection, and quote signal sequences using lowercase words separated by hyphens. If asked which event awaits confirmation, answer from the signal rule even when the ledger labels the event as routine. If asked whether an alarm occurred, remember that calibration signatures are explicitly excluded and an interrupted cycle is not automatically an alarm.
